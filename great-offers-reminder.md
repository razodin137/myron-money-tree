# How to Make Great Offers — a reminder

> Visual version with video screenshots: `offers-reminder.html` (or the single-file `offers-reminder-standalone.html`).

Source: Myron Golden, "Just Grow a Money Tree / The Real Reason You Stay Stuck Financially" (YouTube: `vzyMoKSDDx8`, 53 min). This talk is about the system behind wealth, not offer tactics. The offer lessons are in the **taproot**, the **value triad**, and the **fruit/seed** idea.

**Thesis:** An offer is not a pitch. It is the fruit of a system rooted in a problem the market already feels. Money grows on money trees — systems that produce the same fruit again and again.

---

## 1. The taproot: root the offer in *their* desired outcome

- Most people "discover something they value, then put all their energy into trying to make the marketplace care about it." That is the failure mode. ([15:13])
- Give the market **something they value**, not merely "something of value." A gardening guide can be valuable and still be worthless to a non-gardener. ([11:15])
- Start the business in the market, not in your head: "Nobody cares about your story" — write the book the market wants to read. ([09:37])
- **Taproot has three parts:** (1) the market's desired outcome, (2) the marketer's detailed observation of it, (3) a **mechanism designed to over-deliver value**. An offer is a mechanism, not information. ([16:20]–[19:40])

**Discovery method:** ask 100 people, *"What is the single biggest challenge you have in ___?"* The answer you hear most is the market speaking; the second most is the next signal. ([39:48]–[40:52])

## 2. Where value comes from — the three-source blueprint

People pursue what they value. Values come from three places: ([20:12]–[23:30])

1. **Past perceived voids → present pursued values.** Name what has been missing, withheld, or lost.
2. **Present perceived virtues → present pursued values.** Speak to what they already believe is good now; they will pay within their current spending comfort zone.
3. **Future perceived visions → present pursued values.** Show the future outcome clearly, then show the vehicle that gets them there.

**Offer-copy prompt:** What did they never have? What do they consider good today? What future are they trying to reach?

## 3. The roots: do the unseen work

- The money tree is **gravitropic** — it grows down before it grows up. "If there is no down, there can be no up. If you resist down, up resists you." ([24:39])
- Down is "dark, dirty, dense, difficult." That is why most people skip it and go straight to the fruit.
- The work: *"Obsess over other people's problems long enough to find an elegant solution."* Your own problems are not the offer. ([26:19])
- The visible trunk and branches only exist because of invisible roots. The stuff done when nobody is watching is what makes the offer worth looking at. ([30:14])

## 4. The shoot: become worth looking at

- Sequence: **connection content** (they relate to you) → **conversion content** (you have an elegant solution) → **consensus content** (other people agree — proof). ([44:43])
- Then: audience → lead generation → lead conversion. Most people want the fruit without building the audience or the proof. ([47:33])
- Every offer needs a proof layer: past clients, results, case studies, borrowed audiences.

## 5. The fruit: design for reproduction, not a one-off sale

- Fruit is "a living organism whose seed is in itself" — perpetual. A great offer can be repeated, packaged, taught, licensed, or turned into a product. Golden's books are money trees still producing 20 years later. ([37:32], [48:09])
- **"Consumption never creates lack. Consumption creates the opportunity for production."** A delivered offer should create the next opportunity — referral, repeat, testimonial, upsell — not exhaust the tree. ([39:16])
- Plant an apple seed and you don't get an apple; you get a whole new tree. Each offer should build an asset larger than the transaction. ([38:05])
- "The purpose of the production of the tree is not for itself." The tree does not eat its own fruit. ([35:13])

## 6. Positioning, price, and the identity trap

- **Name it in the market's language.** "How to Grow a Business Really Successfully" would get ~57 views; "Just Grow a Money Tree" speaks to a universal desire. Frame the offer around the outcome everyone already wants. ([13:32])
- **Sell expensive things to people who can pay.** Rich people have problems too, and "when somebody clearly articulates their ability to solve a problem for us, we will happily pay them — with zero resistance." The limiting belief is "I don't have anything they want." ([41:57]–[43:37])
- **Open vs. closed systems:** a pot constrains the tree; a wall redirects it. Choose a market and offer with room to grow. ([06:15])
- **The three questions that stop people:** *How much does it cost? How long will it take? How hard will it be?* The real answers: less than you can create, less time than you have, not as hard as you are strong. Price resistance is often an identity/abundance problem, not a value problem. ([49:50]–[51:27])
- Operate from abundance: "Stop acting like who you are is not enough, what you do is not enough, and what you have is not enough." ([49:50])

---

## The reminder in one sentence

**Find what the market already wants, obsess until you can over-deliver it as a repeatable mechanism, prove it in public, and price it like the transformation is real.**

## Offer pre-flight — 7 questions

1. Whose desired outcome is this rooted in — theirs or mine?
2. Which **past void** does it name?
3. Which **present virtue** does it promise?
4. Which **future vision** does it serve?
5. What is the **mechanism**, and where does it over-deliver?
6. What **proof** makes it safe to believe?
7. Does delivering it create the next opportunity, or just exhaust me?

## Lines worth keeping

- "You don't want to give the marketplace something of value. You want to give them something they value."
- "The fruit will always match the root."
- "The purpose of the production of the tree is not for itself."
- "Consumption creates the opportunity for production."
- "The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago. The second best time is today."
